How to use LeafGuard

LeafGuard looks at a photo of a plant leaf and tells you whether it looks healthy or which disease it most likely has. You don't need any technical knowledge to use it.

Which plants are supported?

  • Tomato: healthy, early blight, late blight, bacterial spot, leaf mold
  • Potato: healthy, early blight, late blight
  • Apple: healthy, apple scab
Photos of other plants will still get an answer, but it will not be meaningful.

Checking a leaf, step by step

  1. Take a good photo. Photograph one leaf, filling most of the picture, in daylight, on a plain background. Hold the phone steady so the photo is sharp.
  2. Open “Check a leaf”. Drag the photo onto the dashed box, or click the box and choose the photo from your device. A small preview appears.
  3. Click “Check this leaf”. The answer appears in a second or two.
  4. Read the result. A green box with ✅ means the leaf looks healthy. A red box with ⚠️ shows the most likely disease.
  5. Tell us if it was right. Click “Yes, correct”, or “No, it's wrong” and choose the right answer. This helps us keep the system accurate.

Understanding the result

  • Confidence: how sure the system is. Above 90% is very sure. Below 60% you'll see a warning, so retake the photo or ask an expert.
  • Other possibilities: the next most likely answers.
  • “Where the model looked”: a colour overlay on your photo. Red areas influenced the decision most; on a diseased leaf these should sit on the spots or lesions.
  • Photo warnings: if your photo is very dark or blurry, you'll be told. Retaking it usually gives a better answer.

Common problems

MessageWhat to do
“That file isn't a photo we can read”Use a JPG, PNG or WEBP photo (not PDF or HEIC).
“That photo is too large”The limit is 5 MB. Use a lower camera resolution or share the photo at “medium” size.
“Cannot reach the LeafGuard service”The service is offline. The status dot (top right) is red. Try again later or contact your administrator.
“The model is not loaded yet”The system is starting up. Wait a minute and try again.

For operators: the Pipeline console

The Pipeline console shows the machine-learning system behind LeafGuard:

  • Pipeline DAG: the data and training steps and how long each took last time.
  • Data drift: whether recent uploads look different from the training photos (e.g. darker or blurrier). Sustained drift automatically starts retraining.
  • Model registry: every trained model version; the one marked production is answering users.
  • Run pipeline now: retrain from scratch. A new model only replaces the current one if it scores at least as well.
  • Roll back model: go back to the previous version (click twice to confirm).
  • Live monitoring: the Grafana dashboard with traffic, response times, errors and drift.
LeafGuard gives guidance only. For important decisions such as spraying or removing plants, confirm with an agronomist.
